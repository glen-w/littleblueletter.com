¡Viva México!
Despite opposition from a powerful fishing industry, the Mexican government this week announced the creation of North America's largest fully protected marine reserve. The sanctuary covers 150,000km² around the Revillagigedo islands, 390km south-west of Baja California. Mexican president Enrique Pena Nieto also announced a ban on mining and the construction of new hotels on the islands.
This bold move not only protects the pristine waters that hundreds of animal and plant species call home (including the mighty manta rays, whales, sea turtles, and sharks), it will also give some of the most overfished species of tuna and mackerel space to recover from the devastating impacts of skyrocketing consumption.
A herd of silky sharks prowling along the coast 

While such announcements are a cause for celebration, only 3% of the ocean is protected by effectively managed marine protected areas, with a paltry 1.5% covered by no-take marine reserves. That means we're still a long way from achieving the ambitious Aichi target of protecting 10% of coastal and marine areas by 2020. Nevertheless, “the journey of a thousand miles begins with one step” (Laozi). 
#OceanOptimism
There is still some hope for the Great Barrier Reef! Scientists have stepped in as environmental matchmakers, collecting coral eggs and sperm from Heron Island’s reef during last November’s coral spawning to produce more than a million larvae. Some of that larvae was reintroduced to the reef and is now growing successfully.

Meanwhile, on land
A new report issued by UN Environment's Executive Director, Towards a Pollution-Free Planet, analyses the impacts pollution has on human health and ecosystems. It paints a rather dark picture (nearly a quarter of all deaths worldwide are due to environmental causes), but also provides solutions and ambition for change.

From the editor
Another bit of good news on coral reefs - some small areas of the Great Barrier Reef happen to be in cool enough waters to be relatively unaffected by rising temperatures, but also close enough to ocean currents to allow for spawning. This gives us some hope that these reefs can help rehabilitate others. The UN Environment Assembly is currently on in Nairobi. BBC's Blue Planet II series continues to astound and amaze.
