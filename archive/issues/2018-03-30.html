What do you think when I say ‘salt fingers’? (Clue: they can be made in the kitchen but have nothing to do with food. Or fingers.) This is what they look like: 
A school experiment by John Mickett, a physical oceanographer at the University of Washington.
 
These funny fingerlike structures are caused by sinking blobs of warm, salty water interleaving with rising cold, fresh water. The process plays a major role in ocean mixing. Learn more about this, and the double diffusion process that causes the phenomenon, here.

#OceanOptimism
A conservation project I’d love to share: an expedition to the Maldives next winter that we are planning together with the Institute of Oceanology in Moscow. The project was born spontaneously after a talk with a friend who had just returned from a holiday the Maldives. She saw a lot of plastic debris, and was horrified to see dead sharks in the water. We plan to visit the islands, take some drone footage, underwater videos, explore the islands, make photographic reports, and take water samples to see the extent of chemical pollution in the water and the impact on the ecosystem. If you’ve been to the Maldives, or you got an idea to share, I’d love to hear from you and take a few tips!
Further reading
  • Alone in the Ocean by Slava Kurilov, a Russian oceanographer who swam three days in a row in the Pacific to escape from his daily routine. His revelations about the ocean he experienced are astounding. 
  • Of Orcas and Men: What Killer Whales Can Teach Us by David Neiwert is an exploration of the science and stories behind the fight against killer whale captivity.
