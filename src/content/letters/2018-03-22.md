<hr/><strong>Keeping your head above water</strong><br/>
A light bulb h<span style="color: rgb(85, 85, 85); font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">anging in a fire station in Livermore, California, has </span>been dimly glowing almost continuously since 1901. Yet today's standard light bulbs last half as long as they did a century ago. Early b<span style="color: rgb(85, 85, 85); font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">ulb manufacturers quickly realised that </span>durable bulbs don't need to be replaced as often, which meant less profit. So they <a href="https://spectrum.ieee.org/tech-history/dawn-of-electronics/the-great-lightbulb-conspiracy">formed a cartel</a> and limited the longevity of bulbs to ensure that sales continued to grow steadily.<br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2018-03-22-1-giphycopie2.gif" style="width: 300px; max-width: 300px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="300"/>
<div style="text-align: center;"><em>The oldest light bulb in the world. Good for the planet, bad for profits.</em></div>
<br/>
It's a quirky story, but my fascination turned sour as I reflected on how the profit motive and <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/business/currency/the-l-e-d-quandary-why-theres-no-such-thing-as-built-to-last">planned obsolescence</a> is the driver behind much environmental destruction. We don't just replace light bulbs any more, most of us change our smart phone every couple of years too.<br/>
<br/>
I began to feel hopeless. One person seems so small in the shadow <span style="color: rgb(85, 85, 85); font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">of the overwhelming challenges facing humanity. Indeed, th</span>ere is increasing recognition that climate change and other environmental concerns can easily <a href="https://www.reuters.com/article/us-usa-climate-depression/concern-over-climate-change-linked-to-depression-anxiety-study-idUSKBN1F738X">lead to depression to anxiety</a>. The American Psychiatry Association has put out a <a href="https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/climate-change-and-mental-health-connections/affects-on-mental-health">policy statement</a> and people are forming <a href="https://grist.org/article/depressed-about-climate-change-theres-a-9-step-program-for-that/" style="font-weight: 400; font-style: normal; font-size: 16px;">support groups</a><span style="color: rgb(85, 85, 85); font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;"> to help stay positive. </span><br/>
<br/>
So how do we stay positive? Three simple ideas:
<ul>
<li>Ignore the bad news. Our digital world bombards us with a lifetime's worth of sadness every day. It <a href="http://time.com/5125894/is-reading-news-bad-for-you/">doesn't do us any good</a>.</li>
<li>Get a daily dose of <a href="https://twitter.com/hashtag/oceanoptimism?ref_src=twsrc%5Egoogle%7Ctwcamp%5Eserp%7Ctwgr%5Ehashtag">#OceanOptimism</a>. Remember that there is still <a href="https://www.rainforest-alliance.org/articles/coptimism">cause for hope</a>.</li>
<li>Look after yourself. Go for a walk, eat something nutritious, take a break, seek help when you need it.</li>
</ul>
