<p><strong>🌊 High Seas update</strong><br/>In March 2023, we let out a big sigh of relief as the High Seas Agreement was finally agreed after 15+ years of talks. The Agreement has now entered into force and more than 💯 States have ratified 👌🏻</p>
<p>Following the usual plethora of PrepComs, political wrangling over where the Secretariat will be (🇧🇪 🇨🇱 🇨🇳?) and how it will all work in practice, we are now sailing toward COP1 (January 2027). The High Seas Alliance has some upcoming webinars to get prepared: </p>
<ul>
<li><a href="https://glenwright.earth/go/#hsa-bbnj-chm-webinar">how the Clearing-House Mechanism will work</a> </li>
<li><a href="https://us06web.zoom.us/webinar/register/WN_YCGw0D-9RcOzqsqHQyMv-YB#/registration">what lies ahead between now and the first Conference of the Parties</a>.</li>
</ul>
<hr/>
<p><strong>🇳🇺 Niue: conservation, priced like an investment</strong><br/>With a population just shy of 1,700 people, Niue's marine area is a little bigger than the UK (or Vietnam, depending on your atlas 🌏). They have protected 100% of that seascape, with 40% designated as strict no-take zones. </p>
<p>The <a href="https://niueoceanwide.com/">Niue Ocean Wide Trust</a> funds protection with an investment product rather than a plea for donations. Instead of passing round a vague hat, it sells sponsors 20 years of protection for the no-take zone at NZ$250 per square kilometre. It seems to be working: a 2023 survey found fish numbers up 60% and top predators up 45%. The project is now a finalist in this year's <a href="https://earthshotprize.org/winners-finalists/2026/">Earthshot Prize</a>.</p>

<p><strong>🇧🇿 Belize: blue bonds, five years on</strong><br/>In 2021, Belize bought back a quarter of its national debt at a 45% discount and put the savings into its coasts and reefs. The Blue Bonds project is expected to raise about US$180m over 20 years. </p>
<p>One of the best bits is local: a $40,000 grant helped the Chunox Fishermen Association train new leaders, collect catch data and get a say in Belize's plan to protect 30% of its ocean. "Nobody is better to protect the resources than the ones who use the resources," says Amisai Arevalo, son of a Chunox fishing family. <a href="https://www.nature.org/en-us/what-we-do/our-insights/perspectives/community-engagement-marine-conservation-belize/">Read more from TNC</a></p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>🇳🇴 Andfjorden</strong><br/>Norway just drew a <a href="https://www.regjeringen.no/en/whats-new/andfjorden-blir-noregs-storste-marine-verneomrade/id3173076/">very large line around Andfjorden</a>. It's the country's biggest marine protected area (1,848.5 km²), with coral gardens, kelp, whales and seabirds. (Fisheries still get a seat at the management table. And it's probably best not to mention seabed mining and offshore oil drilling 🤫).</p>
