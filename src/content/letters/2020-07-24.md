<hr style="color: rgb(85, 85, 85);"/><span style="color: rgb(85, 85, 85); font-weight: bolder; font-size: 16px;">Supertrees between land and sea</span><br/>
This Sunday marks the <a href="https://en.unesco.org/commemorations/mangroveday">International Day for the Conservation of the Mangrove Ecosystem</a> <span style="color: rgb(85, 85, 85); font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">🌱🌿 </span><span style="font-size: 1em;">Mangroves offer myriad of benefits to both people and nature: they act as a barrier between villages and the ocean in the face of tropical storms and rising sea levels, they are a vital habitat for creatures great and small, and they provide a foundation for coastal livelihoods and food security for isolated communities.</span>
<p dir="ltr" role="presentation" style="text-align: center;"><img src="/images/letters/inferred-2020-07-24-1-mangroves1.png" style="width: 500px; max-width: 500px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="500"/></p>
<p dir="ltr" role="presentation" style="text-align: center;"><em>The vibrant mangroves of southwest Madagascar</em><br/>
<span style="color: rgb(85, 85, 85); font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400; text-align: center;">© </span>Louise Jasper, Blue Ventures</p>
<p dir="ltr" role="presentation">But mangroves have one more trick up their sleeve… they can absorb CO2 from the atmosphere, making them one of the most effective natural climate change solutions available. Despite this, they are being deforested at an alarming rate, threatening both the lives of the communities who depend on them and the fate of our environment.<br/>
<br/>
New <a href="https://blueventures.org/new-research-tackling-the-climate-emergency-through-locally-led-mangrove-conservation/">research</a> from Madagascar proves the huge value of mangroves, and their ability to lock away CO2 in the face of the climate crisis – supporting international calls to emphasise their conservation. Even reforested mangrove soils can take decades to restore to health, and to counterbalance the increased amount of CO2 lost from these degraded soils, at least 4.5 hectares of mangroves would need to be replanted for every 1 hectare deforested to come close to matching the CO2 stored over a ten year period. Keeping mangroves in the mud must be a priority!</p>

<div dir="ltr"><strong>#OceanOptimism</strong></div>
<ul>
<li role="presentation"><a href="https://oceanpanel.org/sites/default/files/2020-07/Ocean%20Panel_Economic%20Analysis_FINAL.pdf">Research published last week</a> shows that mangrove restoration offers a quick way to generate jobs, and provides returns of about $3 for every $1 spent, in the form of more productive fisheries and storm protection.</li>
<li role="presentation">​Beyond the fringes of the mangroves, community-led marine reserves in southwest Madagascar have <a href="https://discover.blueventures.org/more-fish-in-the-sea/#section_1">proven to increase fish biomass by 189%</a> - or in other words, community conservation means plenty more fish in the sea! 🐟🐠🐡</li>
</ul>
