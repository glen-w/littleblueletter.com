<div style="font-weight: 400; font-style: normal; font-size: 16px; color: rgb(85, 85, 85);"><span style="font-weight: bolder; font-style: normal; font-size: 16px;">Sand is the new oil</span>
<div dir="ltr">Sand, you would think, is everywhere. Deserts are full of it, the seabed is covered with it, and we use it in large quantities for construction, glass making, and land reclamation projects. But a recent <a href="https://www.unenvironment.org/news-and-stories/press-release/rising-demand-sand-calls-resource-governance">UN Environment report </a>has highlighted that sand is becoming a scarce commodity and that we are spending our sand ‘budget’ faster than we can produce it responsibly.<br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2019-06-06-1-capturedecran2019-06-07a141222.png" style="width: 600px; max-width: 600px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="600"/>Moreover, sand mining threatens the health of our environment and <a href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/2019/04/sand-mining-cambodia-the-lost-world-film/">ways of life, from Cambodia to Nigeria. </a>Sand mining can have disastrous consequences for coasts, rivers and mangroves, where it leads to affect fish and crustaceans stocks. In order to curb irresponsible and illegal extraction, the report suggests increasing dialogue between key players and stakeholders in the sand value chain based on transparency and accountability.</div>

<hr/><span style="font-weight: bolder; font-style: normal; font-size: 16px;">Marine gender equality</span><br/>
<span style="font-size: 1em;">For World Oceans Day – the </span><a href="http://bit.ly/2InaLIW" style="font-size: 1em;">Institute of Marine Engineering, Science &amp; Technology</a><span style="font-size: 1em;"> (IMarEST) is announcing the launch of an </span><a href="http://bit.ly/2K1YqgA" style="font-size: 1em;">IMarEST Women’s Network</a><span style="font-size: 1em;">. The network aims to build a forum for gender equality within the marine industry to help inspire the next generation of female professionals into the marine sector, especially into decision making roles that affect women more greatly than men – such as </span><a href="http://bit.ly/2WiX0jv" style="font-size: 1em;">disaster response</a><span style="font-size: 1em;">. By undertaking a range of activities, it hopes to address a number of the historical and social disadvantages affecting women entering the industry.<br/>

<hr/><span style="font-weight: bolder; font-style: normal; font-size: 16px;">From waste to waves</span>
<div dir="ltr">It’s time to kick cigarette buds’ butt! 31 May marks <a href="https://www.who.int/tobacco/wntd/en/">World No Tobacco Day</a> (the UN loves creating days for everything). We know that cigarette are disastrous for human health, but did we pay attention to the fact that out of the 5.6 trillion cigarettes that are produced worldwide every year, more than two thirds end up littered into our environment and oceans? Fun fact: the majority of them come with filters made of cellulose acetate – a form of plastic which takes more than a decade to decompose. The San Diego based <a href="https://www.cigwaste.org/about-us">Cigarette Butt Pollution Project </a>aims to fight this cigarette waste through research and engagement, while industrial designer Taylor Lane makes <a href="https://www.thecigarettesurfboard.com/backstory">Ciggy Boards</a> - surfboards out of littered cigarette buds to ride the wave against waste.</div>
