<hr/><strong><span style="font-weight: bolder;">Ocean update</span></strong>
<ul>
<li>Scientists conducting an <a href="https://www.ecori.org/narragansett-bay/2019/12/23/aerial-survey-of-marine-monument-documents-rare-unusual-species">aerial survey</a> of the US <a href="https://earthjustice.org/features/explainer-marine-national-monument">Northeast Canyons and Seamounts Marine National Monument</a> recently observed a Chilean devil ray and four True's beaked whales. Chilean devil rays don't need to surface for air, so they are rarely seen.</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div style="">
<div style="font-weight: 400; text-align: center;"><em>Chilean devil rays can feed at depths of up to 6,000 feet.<br/>
Adults can grow to a width of some 12 feet.  (New England Aquarium)</em><br/>
 </div>
<div style="font-weight: 400; margin-left: 40px;">True's beaked whales were first identified in 1913 and have only been observed a handful of times since, making it one of the rarest whale species. The team also observed nine fin whales and two sperm whales, both endangered species.</div>
<div style="font-weight: 400; text-align: center;"></div>

<ul style="font-weight: 400;">
<li>The test being used to diagnose the novel coronavirus—and other pandemics like AIDS and SARS—was developed with the help of an <a href="https://www.whoi.edu/news-insights/content/finding-answers-in-the-ocean/">enzyme isolated from a microbe found in marine hydrothermal vents</a>.</li>
<li>The Seychelles <a href="https://www.nature.org/en-us/about-us/where-we-work/africa/stories-in-africa/seychelles-conservation-commitment-comes-to-life/">hit its goal</a> of protecting 30% of its waters!</li>
</ul>
