Before the boring bits, let me say this: <em>Blue Planet II</em> is truly awesome. The first episode, was a masterclass in science and cinematography. If you are in the UK, make BPII your Sunday evening ritual; if you aren't in the UK, you can still see the stunning <a href="https://youtu.be/_38JDGnr0vA">trailer</a>, check out some <a href="http://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p04tjbtx/clips">clips</a>, and listen to the accompanying <a href="http://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/p05jv4yz/episodes/downloads">podcast</a>. The BBC has also setup a <a href="http://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/articles/1FlfcGGKPSWv3m7JdfBT5dv/get-involved-with-ocean-conservation">website</a> giving some conservation pointers to those inspired by the show. <br/>
<br/>
The Convention on Migratory Species wrapped up last week in Manila - the largest meeting in its 38-year history. A <a href="http://www.cms.int/sites/default/files/cms_cop12_pr_closing_e_0.pdf">record number of commitments</a> were made; three species of shark and three species of ray will receive greater protection. The Philippines was commended for its efforts to protect whale sharks, which are <a href="http://www.pewtrusts.org/en/multimedia/video/2017/migratory-sharks-sustain-livelihoods-in-the-philippines-and-worldwide">essential to livelihoods</a> in the region.<br/>
<br/>
Things moved more slowly in London, where the International Maritime Organisation (IMO), "<a href="http://www.imo.org/en/MediaCentre/PressBriefings/Pages/28-Progress_made_in_developing_GHG_strategy.aspx">made progress</a> in starting to shape a draft initial IMO greenhouse gas strategy". The aim is to reach consensus at the next meeting and to agree short-term measures between 2018-2023. Hardly groundbreaking, but it's a start.<br/>
<br/>
A couple of weeks back, a <a href="https://www.facebook.com/BluePlanetSoc/photos/a.220545757982070.48080.218744051495574/1478506888852611/?type=3&amp;theater">shocking photo</a> of plastic pollution in Honduras made the rounds online. Following a flurry of social media attention, the President of Honduras has appointed a task force and Guatemala, from where most of the litter comes, committed $2 million to building a 'waste-trap' to stem the flow. In the meantime, Honduras is asking for compensation. Perhaps we need an <a href="http://www.pnas.org/content/114/38/9994.full.pdf">international agreement</a> on marine plastic pollution?
