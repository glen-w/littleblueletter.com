<div style="font-weight: 400; font-style: normal; font-size: 16px; color: rgb(85, 85, 85);"><strong>Kriller move to protect the Antarctic</strong><br/>
In an unprecedented move, a huge group of krill fishing companies have <a href="https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/2018/07/antarctica-krill-fishermen-protect-penguin-colonies-peninsula-environment/">committed to</a> supporting ocean sanctuaries in the Antarctic and will <a href="https://www.bbc.com/news/science-environment-44771943">voluntarily stop fishing</a> in sensitive waters slated for protection. <span style="font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">D</span><span style="font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">ecisions made <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2018/jun/13/antarctic-ice-melting-faster-than-ever-studies-show" style="font-size: 16px;">in the next decade</a> are vital for the future of the Antarctic, </span><span style="font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">so this is great news for penguins and for accelerating political efforts to protect the world’s last wilderness.</span></div>
<div style="font-weight: 400; font-style: normal; font-size: 16px; color: rgb(85, 85, 85);"><img src="/images/letters/inferred-2018-07-13-1-javierwithpenguinatdefra.jpg" style="width: 276px; max-width: 276px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="276"/><span style="font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">Krill is the bedrock of the Antarctic foodweb and krill fishing companies have a powerful voice in negotiations. </span><span style="font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">This precautionary approach was <a href="https://brc.org.uk/news/2018/why-retailers-have-brokered-a-new-agreement-to-protect-antarctic-wildlife">welcomed by major retailers</a>, who have faced <a href="https://www.greenpeace.org.uk/victory-holland-barrett-ditches-krill-oil-protect-antarctic/">public pressure</a> regarding krill oil sourced from sensitive waters.</span></div>
<div style="font-weight: 400; font-style: normal; font-size: 16px; color: rgb(85, 85, 85);">
<hr/><strong>No country for cold men</strong><br/>
With polar scientists gathering in Cambridge to workshops on managing the Antarctic Ocean, Greenpeace installed an <a href="http://medium.com/@GreenpeaceUK/28a8d33ad485">immersive geo-dome</a> in the city, projecting stunning footage of the Antarctic seabed thriving with life alongside messages supporting Antarctic protection from people around the world. Oscar-winning actor and penguin fan Javier Bardem stopped by to meet with scientists, describing why he’s <a href="http://edition.cnn.com/2018/01/24/opinions/saving-the-antarctic-bardem-opinion/index.html">joined a movement</a> of 1.7 million people globally to create the world’s largest ocean sanctuary. He even fitted in a meeting with the UK’s Environment Secretary (with a <a href="http://twitter.com/BardemAntarctic/status/1016352322547605506">penguin in tow</a>, naturally).<br/>

<hr/><strong>High time for the high seas</strong><br/>
Elsewhere in international waters, Enric Sala makes a <a href="http://www.ted.com/talks/enric_sala_let_s_turn_the_high_seas_into_the_world_s_largest_nature_reserve">compelling case</a> for protecting the high seas as new research shows the majority of high seas fisheries are <a href="http://news.nationalgeographic.com/2018/06/high-seas-fishing-subsidies-oceans-science/">only economically viable with government subsidies</a> and exploited labour.

<hr/><span style="font-weight: bolder;">Life in plastic</span><br/>
If we’re going to stop sealife choking on plastic, we need to tackle the problem at source - that means calling on companies to help end the era of single-use plastic. Check out campaigns across the world that are helping to <a href="https://twitter.com/hashtag/breakfreefromplastic">#BreakFreeFromPlastic</a>. Be sure to also check out <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2018/jul/09/disabled-person-plastic-straws-baby-wipes">this article</a>, from disability rights campaigner Penny Paper, about the challenges some people may face in giving up plastic: "If there’s going to be a ban, let governments and manufacturers come up with decent, affordable, green alternatives for us all.”
