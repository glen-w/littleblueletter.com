<div style="font-style: normal; font-size: 16px; color: rgb(85, 85, 85);"><strong>Slip slop slap</strong><br/>
As May long weekends mark the beginning of summer in the Northern Hemisphere, it’s time to stock up on the SPF. But conventional sunscreen can be toxic to marine life. Even a drop of oxybenzone can damage delicate corals, and researchers estimate that we about 14,000 tons of sunscreen end up in reefs each year. So this year I’m searching for an ocean-friendly alternative (though it’ll be hard to say goodbye to the iconic smell of Coppertone).<br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2018-05-26-1-docx-littleblueletterdraft-d3617b8f5fe36.png" style="width: 334px; max-width: 334px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="334"/>
<div style="font-weight: 400; text-align: center;"><em>The inner reef of Oahu's Hanauma Bay in Hawaii. Credit: Caleb Jones/AP via NPR.</em></div>
<br/>
Hawaii has <a href="https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2018/05/02/607765760/hawaii-approves-bill-banning-sunscreen-believed-to-kill-coral-reefs" style="font-weight: 400;">passed a law</a> that will prohibit the sale of sunscreens containing oxybenzone from 2021. In the meantime, you can choose one of these <a href="http://www.travelandleisure.com/style/beauty/reef-safe-sunscreen#manda" style="font-weight: 400;">reef-safe options</a>. (And if you really miss the Coppertone, <a href="http://cbihateperfume.com/shop/perfumes-a-to-z/101" style="font-weight: 400;">this perfume</a> has you covered).

<hr style="font-weight: 400;"/>
<div><strong>#OceanOptimism</strong><br/>
Overfishing is one of the biggest threats to the world’s ocean, with the UN Food and Agriculture Organisation (FAO) estimating that <a href="http://www.fao.org/documents/card/en/c/2c8bcf47-2214-4aeb-95b0-62ddef8a982a">30% of fish stocks are overexploited</a>. Nonetheless, a report released this week by the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) found that the number of overfished stocks in the US is at an <a href="https://www.undercurrentnews.com/2018/05/17/noaa-us-overfished-stocks-at-all-time-low/">all-time low</a>.</div>
<div><img src="/images/letters/inferred-2018-05-26-2-fishing.png" style="text-align: center; width: 374px; max-width: 374px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="374"/></div>
<div>Choosing sustainable seafood can help keep the good news coming. Look out for ecolabels that have been accredited by the <a href="http://www.ourgssi.org/about-2/gssi-story/">Global Sustainable Seafood Initiative</a> – an independent benchmarking organisation that recognises credible labels. So far only five ecolabels have made the cut, including the <a href="https://20.msc.org/what-you-can-do/10-reasons-to-choose-the-blue-fish-label">Marine Stewardship Council (MSC) blue fish tick</a>, the Alaska Responsible Fisheries Management (RFM) Certification Program and the GLOBALG.A.P Aquaculture Certification System.

<hr/><strong>More #OceanOptimism!</strong><br/>
Madagascar is a hotspot for whale sharks, according to new research. Over 85 individuals were identified in one season. "No one thought there were that many", Stella Diamant of the Madagascar Whale Shark Project <a href="http://www.bbc.co.uk/news/science-environment-44151782">told the BBC</a>.<br/>

<div style="text-align: center;"><em>A researcher and a whale shark off the coast of Nosy Be island near Madagascar. In Malagasy, whale sharks are called marokintana, meaning many stars. Credit: Simon J. Pierce.</em>
<div style="text-align: left;">
<hr/><strong>From the editor</strong><br/>
The FAO’s statistics on fisheries come from countries themselves, so they are prone to underestimation. True catches may be <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms10244">much higher</a> than we previously thought. This week, Ireland's upper house (Seanad) <a href="https://www.irishtimes.com/news/environment/seanad-motion-to-call-for-marine-protected-areas-off-irish-coasts-1.3504871">supported a motion</a> calling for the designation of a network of marine protected areas covering up to 50% of Irish waters. The motion is non-binding, but the Minister has committed to tabling legislation in the coming weeks. Humans have been fishing for <a href="https://www.livescience.com/17186-oldest-fish-hooks-early-humans.html">at least 42,000 years</a>.
