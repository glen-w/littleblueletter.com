<div style="text-align: left;"><strong>Cyber pirates</strong><br/>
The shipping industry carries around <a href="http://www.ics-shipping.org/shipping-facts/shipping-and-world-trade">90% of world trade</a>. Almost any product you can think of follows a well-oiled distribution chain: factory, truck, ship, truck, consumer. Any disruption to this chain results in significant economic loss for companies, and ultimately the customer. While maritime piracy is traditionally enemy number one, costing up to <a href="https://worldmaritimenews.com/archives/134829/annual-global-cost-of-piracy-measured-in-billions/">$16 billion per year</a>, new technologies are bringing more advanced threats.<br/>
<br/>
Last year, the <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2017/jun/28/notpetya-ransomware-attack-ukraine-russia">NotPetya ransomware</a> attack <a href="https://www.newsdeeply.com/oceans/articles/2018/02/05/hacked-at-sea-concerns-grow-over-lax-cybersecurity-for-ships-ports">caused chaos</a> at the Port of New York and New Jersey. Trucks waited up to 8 hours to pick up goods as one of the world's busiest ports was forced to revert to manual systems for managing incoming cargo. The episode was a jarring wake-up call to the shipping industry.<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2018-01-31-1-docx-littleblueletterjanuary2018gwedit-7.jpg" style="font-weight: normal; font-style: normal; font-size: 16px; outline-color: rgb(236, 33, 38); width: 328px; max-width: 328px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="328"/></div>
<div>
<div style="text-align: center;"><em>Queue forming outside Port Newark.<br/>
Delays are especially damaging to truck drivers, often paid per trip completed, rather than hourly.</em></div>
<div style="text-align: left;"><br/>
Three years ago, the Port of Los Angeles established a Cybersecurity Operations Center, which has since handled <a href="http://docs.house.gov/meetings/HM/HM00/20171030/106517/HHRG-115-HM00-Wstate-SerokaE-20171030.pdf">20 million-plus cyber intrusion attempts</a>. Meanwhile, cyber-security experts have shown that it is possible to <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/world/2017/may/05/cybercrime-billionaires-superyacht-owners-hacking">hijack a billionaire's superyacht</a> from your laptop.<br/>
<br/>
Clearly the industry's gaping security holes won't be fixed by a Windows XP update.

There are many things you can do to stop itty bitty bits of plastic ending up in our ocean. Start  in the bathroom: pass on the plastic shampoo bottles by using shampoo bars. Everything you need to know <a href="http://www.jacalynbeales.com/journal/how-to-find-the-right-shampoo-bar">here</a>. On the bathroom sustainability warpath? <hr/><strong>From the editor</strong><br/>
We published a comment piece in Nature last week which I am super excited about: <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-018-01594-1">Protect the neglected half of our blue planet</a>. I am currently reading <a href="http://amzn.to/2CoNq4J"><em>The Box</em></a> by Marc Levinson. Malcom McLean, who's parents didn't have enough money to send him to university, used their meagre savings to buy him a truck. He turned that truck into a freight company, and ultimately pioneered the intermodal shipping container. There are now more than 20 million containers in circulation. The world's <a href="https://www.maritime-executive.com/article/oocl-hong-kong-achieves-guinness-world-record">biggest container ship</a> can carry 21,000 of them.<br/>
