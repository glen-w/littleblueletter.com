<div style="font-size: 16px;">
<hr/><span style="font-weight: bolder;">Ocean update</span><br/>
It is quite difficult to talk positively about what is <a href="https://phys.org/news/2020-01-blue-colossal-human-pressure-ocean.html">happening deep down on the dark ocean sea floor</a>. As companies rush to get hold of precious metals 2,500 meters under the sea, a plethora of marine life - from <a href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/allenelizabeth/2020/02/03/how-your-smartphone-may-be-destroying-the-deep-oceanand-its-valuable-microbes/#523f24fb4405">microbes </a>to <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2020/jan/25/race-for-seabed-threat-to-oceans">snails that grow shells made of iron</a> - is under threat.<br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2020-02-07-1-ezgif-5-43b7ca403886.gif" style="width: 466px; max-width: 466px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="466"/>
<p dir="ltr">Many imagine the seabed to be just a vast expanse of sand, but in fact, it is a diverse and dynamic landscape just as we find it on land: mountains, ragged canyons, hot springs, undersea lakes…and rich in metals such as cobalt.  </p>
<p dir="ltr">The International Seabed Authority is tasked with <a href="https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2020/01/20000-feet-under-the-sea/603040/">managing seabed mining and mitigating environmental impacts</a>. But many are concerned that the Authority is not doing its job properly and fear that exploitation regulations, due to be concluded this year, will fall short.<br/>
<br/>
The <a href="https://phys.org/news/2020-01-blue-colossal-human-pressure-ocean.html">good news</a> is that the area protected from some exploitation has increased exponentially, and renewable energies such as offshore wind farming have reached commercial viability which allows us to reduce our reliance on fossil fuels. Yet it is also new technologies that will require batteries which are made up of metals found on the ocean floor - a conundrum.</p>
