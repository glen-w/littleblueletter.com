<p><strong>🌊 Sea what's been happening?</strong><br/>The Ocean agenda has been making waves all the way to Antalya!</p>
<p>First port of call: Bonn in June, where the <a href="https://unfccc.int/documents/660207">UNFCCC's Ocean and Climate Change Dialogue</a>, co-facilitated by Sivendra Michael of Fiji and Ulrik Lenaerts of Belgium, got countries talking about ocean priorities in their climate plans, money, and how the climate, biodiversity and ocean agendas fit together. Its key messages, published in August, ask for ocean measures in national climate plans, finance that is "accessible, predictable and fit for purpose" for small islands and least developed countries, and better links with the new High Seas Treaty. Above all, it wants the ocean treated as a cross-cutting priority across the climate talks, with a multi-year roadmap to keep it there. 🗺️</p>
<p>Next stop: Trabzon on the Black Sea in September. The Turkish Presidency gathered ministers for its <a href="https://cop31.tr/news-detail/cop31-launches-marine-litter-action-platform-in-trabzon">Blue Dialogue</a>, launching a Marine Litter Action Platform and calling for real ocean outcomes at COP31. The COP31 president reminded everyone that "the seas have no passports."</p>

<hr/>
<p><strong>🐚 Strength in unity</strong><br/>At the Pre-COP Leaders' Plenary in Tuvalu last month, Prime Minister Feleti Teo wore a Taku Pakasoa, a mother-of-pearl necklace symbolising strength in unity. It gave its name to a <a href="https://unfccc.int/documents/661118">declaration</a> signed by nearly 30 countries, recognising ocean warming, acidification and sea-level rise, and calling for more ocean science.</p>
<img src="/images/letters/261113_funafuti_atoll.jpg" alt="Funafuti Atoll from the air" width="400" style="display:block;width:400px;max-width:100%;height:auto;margin:0 auto;"/>
<p style="font-size:12px;line-height:1.4;color:#666666;margin:8px 0 0 0;text-align:center;"><em>Funafuti Atoll, Tuvalu. Credit: Gabriella Jacobi</em></p>
<p>Then the Pacific Pre-COP in Nadi, Fiji in October put the ocean front and centre, and Türkiye with a commitment to work toward an Antalya Ocean Declaration. The COP31 Presidencies name the ocean-climate nexus one of the five pillars of their Pacific Partnership. Peter Thomson, UN Special Envoy for the Ocean, stated that "never again should Mother Ocean, the planet's primary climate regulator, be consigned to the back rows of deliberations." Front-row seat, please? The pieces are on the board. 🧩</p>

<hr/>
<p><strong>🗺️ All currents lead to Antalya</strong><br/>With the last stop of the year at Antalya, the presidency hopes to make this the first true Blue COP, with an Antalya Ocean Declaration on the table. <a href="https://cop31.tr/action-plan/oceans">Oceans and Seas</a> is one of the ten Action Agenda priorities. The presidency's Oceans and Seas agenda has four big asks: get seagrass, salt marshes and mangroves into national climate plans, protect coasts from acidification and marine heatwaves, share more ocean data for early warning, and back coastal communities facing rising seas. Not bad for a sea that soaks up a quarter of our CO₂ 🌱</p>

<p>In the formal talks, countries are pushing for a plan to implement ocean measures, and the cover decision is the likeliest place for it to land. The reality check: for now the negotiations are a black box, and we hope to get some updates in Week 2 of COP on whether Ocean makes it into the text. Fins crossed. 🐠</p>
