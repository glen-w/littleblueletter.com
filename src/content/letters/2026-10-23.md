<p><strong>🌍 Biodiversity COP17: the halfway check-up</strong><br/>The UN Biodiversity Conference opened Monday in Yerevan (Armenia 🇦🇲) and runs until 30 October, with nearly 200 countries reviewing progress on the 23 <a href="https://www.cbd.int/gbf/targets">targets</a> agreed four years ago. The big one? A global pledge to protect 30% of the planet's land and oceans by 2030.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://www.aa.com.tr/en/greenline/climate-change/can-the-world-still-protect-30-of-the-planet-by-2030/1831956">ocean report card</a> is mixed: about 10% of the Ocean is protected (23.3% in national waters; just 1.7% on the high seas). Oceania leads the way with 41%!</p>

<hr/>
<p><strong>🦑 57 new species from one canyon</strong><br/>Remember the live-streamed “Patrick Star” lookalike from Argentina’s deep-seabed? Taxonomists have now worked through the samples from the Mar del Plata Canyon and named 57 new species and 5 new genera in just two weeks. Highlights include “Loki the Sea Cucumber,” named for its horn-like appendages. Every copepod they examined was new to science. <a href="https://schmidtocean.org/57-new-marine-species-submarine-canyons/">All the data</a> is open-access on the Ocean Census platform.</p>

<p><strong>🧴 Plastics treaty: if diplomacy stalls, follow the market</strong><br/>The UN Plastic Treaty negotiations have been stuck for a while. Good news: There is a new chair to the process who promises to speed things up. Bad news?: This may come at the expense of an ambitious Treaty.</p>
<p>The next round of formal negotiations are planned to resume in March 2027. But there’s another lever: countries backing an ambitious treaty make up 20 to 40% of the global plastics market, which may be enough to <a href="https://www.plasticsnews.com/public-policy/pn-plastics-treaty-market-power-strategy/">change the industry</a>.</p>

<p><strong>🏭 Meanwhile, on land</strong><br/>Europe is about to stop shipping its plastic problem elsewhere. From 21 November, the EU <a href="https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/waste-and-recycling/waste-shipments/plastic-waste-shipments_en">halts all plastic waste exports</a> to non-OECD countries. The new rules also add mandatory tracking for hazardous or hard-to-recycle waste. For years, “recycled” European plastic has too often ended up dumped or burned in countries with weaker waste systems. Now it has to be dealt with closer to home.</p>

<hr/>
<p><strong>🌊 Ocean Optimism</strong><br/>The Bezos Earth Fund announced <a href="https://fortune.com/2026/09/30/jeff-bezos-lauren-sanchez-bezos-earth-fund-25-million-pacific-10-billion-commitment-deadline-2030/">$25M in new grants</a> to protect more than 625,000 square miles of ocean across the Cook Islands, Kiribati, Papua New Guinea, Tonga and Vanuatu, including PNG’s first large offshore MPA. A grant to Rare will also help Pacific governments draft some of the first high seas protection proposals under the new treaty.</p>

<p><strong>✏️ A note from the editor</strong><br/>Some bonus bits that caught my eye this week:</p>
<ul>
<li>🪸 <strong>Coral recovery via "tissue bridges"</strong>: <a href="https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aeh1580">Researchers found</a> that briefly touching a healthy coral fragment to a bleached colony of the same genotype lets their tissues fuse and transfer symbiotic algae, kick-starting full self-recovery in as little as 3–7 days.</li>
</ul>
