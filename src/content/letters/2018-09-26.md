<div style="font-style: normal; font-size: 16px; color: rgb(85, 85, 85);"><b>Deeply surprising</b><br/>
The <a href="http://dsbs2018.org/">Deep-Sea Biology Symposium</a> took place in California last week, with researchers from around the world showcasing their latest finds. In areas once considered devoid of life or scientific interest, <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-018-06771-w">we now find</a> "Gummy squirrels”, single-celled organisms the size of softballs, strange worms, fossil beds of whales that lived up to 16 million years ago, and an <a href="https://www.newsdeeply.com/oceans/articles/2018/08/31/in-photos-exploring-the-deep-sea-secrets-of-the-american-deep-south?utm_source=LittleBlueLetter">incredible array of fish and corals</a>.<br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2018-09-26-1-ezgifcom-optimize.gif" style="width: 374px; max-width: 374px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="374"/>
<div style="text-align: center;"><em>Red shrimp, a rattail fish and crustaceans called amphipods.<br/>
Credit: DeepCCZ project</em></div>
<div style="text-align: left;"><br/>
<span style="font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">The International Seabed Authority has already issued licenses to 29 corporations and state-backed companies to explore the deep sea for minerals and is aiming to have exploitation regulations in place by 2020 (developed through a notoriously <a href="https://www.newsdeeply.com/oceans/articles/2017/09/06/seabed-mining-the-24-people-who-could-decide-the-fate-of-the-deep-ocean">secretive</a> process). R</span><span style="font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">esearchers say that <a href="https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/06/170626124544.htm">devastating impacts</a> are</span><span style="font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;"> </span><span style="font-size: 16px; font-style: normal; font-weight: 400;">inevitable and irrevocable, and many are </span>now <a href="https://www.openchannels.org/news/news/european-parliament-calls-moratorium-deep-sea-mining">calling for a moratorium</a>.<br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2018-09-26-2-ezgif-1-b8524c2400.gif" style="width: 312px; max-width: 312px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="312"/></div>
<div style="text-align: center;"><em>A Chaunacops anglerfish<br/>
Credit: NOAA Office of Ocean Exploration and Research</em></div>
<hr style="font-weight: 400;"/>
<div style="text-align: left;"><b>Ocean leadership</b><br/>
<span style="font-weight: 400; font-size: 16px; font-style: normal;">The UK announced this week that it will support a goal of <a href="http://lewispugh.com/30oceanprotection/">protecting 30% of the global ocean</a>, a target previously suggested as necessary by scientific studies. Elsewhere, the i</span>naugural meeting of the <a href="https://www.regjeringen.no/en/aktuelt/opening-of-the-inaugural-meeting-of-the-high-level-panel-on-building-a-sustainable-ocean-economy/id2611798/">High-level Panel on Building a Sustainable Ocean Economy</a> took place. Norwegian Prime Minister Erna Solberg, whose country is spearheading the initiative, said: "As the only ocean policy body consisting of serving world leaders, we have the authority and determination needed to trigger, amplify and accelerate action for ocean protection and production... Our goal is to advance a new contract that will both protect the oceans and optimise their value to humankind."  </div>

<div style="text-align: left;"><span style="font-weight: bolder; font-style: normal; font-size: 16px;"><strong>Octopuses in ecstasy</strong></span><br/>
<span style="font-size: 16px; font-style: normal;">"Before the drug, they explored the chamber with the other octopus very tentatively. They mashed themselves against one wall, very slowly extended one arm, touched the [other animal], and went back to the other side... But when they had MDMA, they had this very relaxed posture. They floated around, they wrapped their arms around the chamber, and they interacted with the other octopus in a much more fluid and generous way. They even exposed their [underside], where their mouth is, which is not something octopuses usually do."</span></div>
