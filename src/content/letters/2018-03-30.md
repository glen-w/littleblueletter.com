<div><strong>In the mix</strong> </div>
<div>A cool new weather website can show you everything from the swell and sea temperature to clouds and currents. Coded by a German kite enthusiast and pilot, <em><a href="https://www.windy.com">Windy</a> </em>is a treat to work with and can give you a better understanding of our planet’s bigger picture.<br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2018-03-30-1-lblunderwaterglowinthedarkgif-source.gif" style="width: 243px; max-width: 243px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="243"/></div>
<div>We already know about some ocean mixing mechanisms, such as the wind and internal waves, but could animal movements also be playing a role? <a href="https://youtu.be/i6aKqhZ_r5c?t=1h7m9s">Here</a> is an exciting talk by John Dabiri, given at the recent Ocean Sciences Meeting in Portland. (Reminds me of a common kids’ assumption that it is the swaying of the trees that causes the wind rather than the other way round. Makes me wonder…)</div>
<div> </div>
<div>What do you think when I say ‘salt fingers’? (Clue: they can be made in the kitchen but have nothing to do with food. Or fingers.) This is what they look like: <br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2018-03-30-2-lblwindy.png" style="width: 262px; max-width: 262px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="262"/></div>
<div style="text-align: center;"><em>A school experiment by John Mickett, a physical oceanographer at the University of Washington.</em></div>
<div> </div>
<div>These funny fingerlike structures are caused by sinking blobs of warm, salty water interleaving with rising cold, fresh water. The process plays a major role in ocean mixing. Learn more about this, and the double diffusion process that causes the phenomenon, <a href="http://www.deepseanews.com/2013/01/ocean-staircases-and-salt-fingers-the-curious-case-of-double-diffusion/">here</a>. </div>

<hr/><strong>#OceanOptimism</strong></div>
<div>A conservation project I’d love to share: an expedition to the Maldives next winter that we are planning together with the Institute of Oceanology in Moscow. The project was born spontaneously after a talk with a friend who had just returned from a holiday the Maldives. She saw a lot of plastic debris, and was horrified to see dead sharks in the water. We plan to visit the islands, take some drone footage, underwater videos, explore the islands, make photographic reports, and take water samples to see the extent of chemical pollution in the water and the impact on the ecosystem. If you’ve been to the Maldives, or you got an idea to share, I’d love to hear from you and take a few tips!</div>
