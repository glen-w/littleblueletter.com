<hr/><span style="font-weight: bolder;">2020: a bumper year for the ocean?</span><br/>
The “<a href="https://www.iisd.org/blog/what-is-cop25-we-have-answers">Blue COP</a>” in December 2019 marked the end of a busy year in ocean policy, which is now high on the international agenda (other highlights for me include the <a href="https://twitter.com/MarinePolicy/status/1177543027843354627">IPCC Special Report</a> and the first <a href="https://enb.iisd.org/oceans/marine-regions-forum/2019/">Marine Regions Forum</a>). The ocean community is gearing up for <a href="https://www.iddri.org/en/publications-and-events/blog-post/2020-super-year-ocean">another big year in 2020</a>, with several important events and political deadlines on the horizon:

<ul>
<li>The second <a href="http://oceanconference.un.org/">UN Ocean Conference</a> (Lisbon, June) will pick up where the first edition left off in 2017, with the goal of <a href="http://undocs.org/en/A/res/73/292">assessing the implementation of SDG14</a> and spurring action. But will it result in ambitious commitments or simply add yet another political declaration and unkept promises?</li>
</ul>
<img src="/images/letters/d49df11f-969c-4f56-bbf2-0fb71de4ed6b.png" style="width: 142px; max-width: 142px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="142"/>
<ul>
<li>The 15th Conference of the Parties of the Convention on Biological Diversity (Kunming, October) will seek to adopt a <a href="https://www.cbd.int/conferences/post2020">Post-2020 Global Biodiversity Framework</a> (update on progress <a href="https://www.iddri.org/en/publications-and-events/blog-post/search-pillars-kunming-what-ambition-biodiversity-cop15-october">here</a>). It could include ambitious ocean targets, including protected area coverage (<a href="https://www.greenpeace.org/international/press-release/21652/30x30-groundbreaking-scientific-study-maps-out-how-to-protect-a-third-of-the-worlds-oceans-by-2030/">30%? by 2030?</a>).</li>
<li>The <a href="https://www.iddri.org/en/publications-and-events/blog-post/high-seas-governance-understanding-upcoming-negotiations-10">long process</a> to negotiate a treaty to protect the <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zwgtn41TpfU">high seas</a>—which covers nearly half of the Earth’s surface and does not belong to any one nation—could conclude this year. All eyes are now on the <a href="https://www.un.org/bbnj/">final round of negotiations</a> at the end of March (UN Headquarters, New York).</li>
</ul>
<img src="/images/letters/25f8e7df-7483-49b1-8c3e-ab0dc29c2dba.jpeg" style="width: 362px; max-width: 362px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="362"/>
<ul>
<li>The World Trade Organization could finally agree to <a href="https://sdg.iisd.org/news/special-envoy-for-oceans-urges-agreement-on-ending-fisheries-subsidies-in-2019/">remove harmful fisheries subsidies</a>.</li>
<li>We will also be closely following the <a href="https://www.iucncongress2020.org/">IUCN World Conservation Congress</a>, the process to develop <a href="https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2020/01/20000-feet-under-the-sea/603040/">seabed mining</a> regulations, the <a href="https://www.ourocean2020.pw/">Our Ocean conference</a>, the <a href="https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/TA-9-2020-0005_EN.html">European Green Deal</a>, and climate <a href="https://www.un.org/esa/forests/events/cop26-unfccc/index.html">COP26</a>.</li>
</ul>
