<div dir="ltr" style=""><b>Protecting a volcanic island oasis</b><br/>
In the South Atlantic Ocean, between the Northeast coast of Brazil and the Northwest coast of Africa, lies an island of natural wonders, where conservationists are adopting innovative management measures to combine sustainable tourism with environmental awareness and conservation.</div>
<p dir="ltr" style="font-weight: 400;">Of volcanic origin, the archipelago of Fernando de Noronha is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, first discovered in the early 1500s by Americo Vespucio. It has been colonized by the Portuguese, fought over by French, English and Dutch, and even used as a prison in the 18th century, when occupation and deforestation took place on the main island.<br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2019-05-10-1-capturedecran2019-05-17a173951.png" style="width: 529px; max-width: 529px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="529"/></p>
<p dir="ltr" style="font-weight: 400; text-align: center;"><em>Two Brothers Mountain</em></p>
<p dir="ltr" style="font-weight: 400;"><span style="font-size: 1em;">The tropical waters are nourished between February and July each year, when rainfall dramatically increases and the ocean bursts into life. </span>The archipelago is a sanctuary to marine life, mainly, tuna, reef-sharks, dolphins, sea turtles reside and breed in the islands. It also represents a migratory hotspot for seabirds and humpback whales. The islands are home to the largest concentration of tropical seabirds in the Western Atlantic.<br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2019-05-10-2-capturedecran2019-05-17a173651.png" style="width: 372px; max-width: 372px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="372"/></p>
<p dir="ltr" style="font-weight: 400; text-align: center;"><em>A walkway through the native rainforest leads to the main tourist sights </em></p>
<p dir="ltr" style="font-weight: 400;">Fernando de Noronha National Park <a href="https://oceangreatideas.com/2014/03/14/ocean-happy-places-fernando-de-noronha-archipelago-national-marine-park-brazil/">has been recognised as an example of successful management and well run Marine Protected Area</a>. T<span style="font-size: 1em;">o effectively safeguard the island's pristine environment, authorities have adopted a range of measures, such as: </span><span style="font-size: 1em;">charging for entry, controlling</span><span style="font-size: inherit;"> tourism and migration flow,</span><span style="font-size: inherit;"> restricting the number of flights that can land in the atoll, strict residence rules (at present approximately 6000 people live there) and <a href="http://www.wwf.org.br/?69342/Fernando-de-Noronha-island-without-plastic-gets-closer-to-reality">banning plastic</a>. <br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2019-05-10-3-capturedecran2019-05-17a180937.png" style="width: 200px; max-width: 200px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="200"/></span></p>
<p dir="ltr" style="font-weight: 400; text-align: center;"><span style="font-size: inherit;"><em>Welcome sign at the airport announcing the plastics ban.</em></span></p>
However, a dramatic change of approach from the new federal government (under President Bolsonaro) may cut funding and slowly shutdown innovative management bodies. Artisanal fishers are also raising concerns that foreign-flagged vessels are fishing in the archipelago waters. And even if plastic is banned locally, global currents can still bring marine litter - a sea turtle was found entangled in plastic packaging from Thailand.<br/>
