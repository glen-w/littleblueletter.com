<div style="font-weight: 400; font-style: normal; color: rgb(85, 85, 85);"><span style="font-size: 16px;"><strong>HFO, GHG, IMO, OMG</strong><br/>
If shipping were a country, it’d be the <a href="https://qz.com/1253874/if-shipping-were-a-country-it-would-the-worlds-sixth-biggest-greenhouse-gas-emitter/">sixth largest emitter</a> of CO2</span><span style="font-size: 13.28px;"> </span>(right between Japan and Germany). So it’s exciting news that last April, the International Maritime Organization (IMO), the United Nations body responsible for developing environmental and safety regulations for international shipping, took steps to reduce the industry’s footprint.<br/>
<img src="/images/letters/inferred-2018-05-18-1-icebreakergif.gif" style="width: 422px; max-width: 422px;display:block;float:none;clear:both;height:auto;max-width:100%;margin:0.75rem auto;" width="422"/>
<div style="text-align: center;"><em>Activists powered washed “IMO DONT SINK PARIS” onto a retaining wall directly across the River Thames from the IMO. (Credit: Campaign Against Climate Change)</em><br/>
 </div>
<div style="text-align: left;">Most notably, the IMO committed to reducing future greenhouse gas emissions by <a href="http://www.imo.org/en/MediaCentre/PressBriefings/Pages/06GHGinitialstrategy.aspx">at least 50% by 2050</a> compared to 2008 levels, which aligns with the goals of the Paris Agreement. It’s not as high as some countries and environmental organizations were seeking, but the “at least” language leaves room for much improvement.<br/>
<br/>
Another win for the ocean and the climate came with the commitment to develop a <a href="https://www.pacificenvironment.org/in-the-news/u-n-committee-moves-toward-banning-heavy-fuel-oil-arctic/">ban on heavy fuel oil</a> (HFO) use in the Arctic, which accounts for <a href="https://www.theicct.org/publications/prevalence-heavy-fuel-oil-and-black-carbon-arctic-shipping-2015-2025">75% of fuel</a> used in the region. HFO is a <a href="https://www.pacificenvironment.org/in-the-news/7-reasons-worried-heavy-fuel-oil/">thick, tar-like sludge</a> that emits black carbon, which can coat ice and speed up melting. In the event of a spill in Arctic waters, HFO would be nearly impossible to clean up.</div>
